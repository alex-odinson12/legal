Privacy Policy

AudioConv for iOS (on the App Store as “Video to MP3 – Ringtone Maker”) · Revision 2026-11-19 · Effective 2026-11-19 · Support

In short

AudioConv takes the sound out of a video or an audio file and saves it as MP3, M4A, WAV or AAC, or as a ringtone. The conversion runs on your iPhone. The app does not send your videos, your audio, the files it makes, or their names and folder paths to us or to the services we use.

Two outside services do receive data from the app, and both start working when you open it, before you pick any file: Adapty runs the purchase of Premium for us, and Mixpanel counts how the app is used – which steps people reach, whether an extraction worked, whether people buy Premium. What they receive is described below; it does not include your files, their names or their contents. There is no switch in the app that turns them off.

There is no account, no name, no e-mail address and no password. There is no advertising in the app, and we do not sell your data. We, the developer, run no server of our own for this app.

A file can still leave your iPhone in ways you control: when you share it, save it to a cloud folder, or when your iPhone backup includes the app's files – see “What the app keeps on your iPhone”.

Your videos, your audio and the files the app makes

AudioConv converts your media on your iPhone. Reading the video, cutting the part you chose, fading it in and out and encoding it are done by Apple's own audio frameworks and, for MP3, by the LAME encoder built into the app. It does not send your media, file names or paths to Adapty or Mixpanel. The app's analytics do include where a file came from (Photos or Files), its size and length as rough ranges, the format and quality you chose, and a rough range for how long the conversion took – see “Usage analytics”.

Where files come from

  • Photos. You pick a video in Apple's photo picker. The picker runs outside the app, so the app never asks for access to your photo library and sees only the video you picked. The app copies that one video into its temporary folder to read it, and deletes the copy when you are done with it, or at the next launch if the app was closed first.
  • Files and iCloud Drive. You pick a video or audio file in Apple's Files picker. The app reads it where it is, without copying it, and only for as long as you are working with it.
  • iCloud. If the video or file you picked is stored in iCloud and not yet on the phone, iOS downloads it from Apple first – the same as opening it in Photos or Files.

The app does not use the camera or the microphone and does not ask for them.

How the new file is named

For a file from Files, the app shows its own name. For a video from Photos, it uses the recording date when the video has one, otherwise the file's modification date or the current date. The new file's name is derived from that displayed name, and you can rename it in the Library.

Sharing a file is your choice

Share hands the file to iOS's Share menu; Save to Files first prepares a temporary copy and lets you pick a folder. Either way, you choose where it goes – a folder in Files, Messages, mail, AirDrop, another app – and the file's contents and name go with it. Making a ringtone works the same way: on iOS 26 you pass the file to iOS through the Share menu, and on iOS 17 and 18 you take it into GarageBand, Apple's app.

Premium and purchases

What is free and what is paid

Your first file is free, in full. It is used up only when an extraction finishes and the file is saved; a cancelled or failed one does not count. After that, extracting sound needs Premium: a weekly or a yearly subscription, or a one-time Lifetime purchase. Files already in your Library stay usable without Premium.

The app records that the free file is used in the system keychain as well as in its own settings. The keychain mark normally survives deleting and reinstalling the app (see “What the app keeps on your iPhone”).

Apple handles the payment

You buy Premium from Apple, in Apple's own purchase sheet. We never see your card number, your billing address or your Apple Account, and the app never asks you for them. What comes back is Apple's confirmation of what was bought, and from then on whether Premium is active, which plan it is, and when a subscription renews or ends. Through Adapty we also receive purchase records: the product, the price and our proceeds, and the store's transaction identifiers.

To cancel a subscription, use Apple's subscription settings on your iPhone (Settings, then your name (Apple Account), then Subscriptions; Apple explains it at support.apple.com/en-us/118428). While Premium is active, the app's Settings also has a Manage Subscription button that opens Apple's sheet. Deleting the app does not cancel anything. Refunds are Apple's to give: ask for one at reportaproblem.apple.com. Restore Purchases in the app's Settings, and Restore on the Premium screen, ask the App Store for a purchase already made with your Apple Account.

Adapty

Adapty (subscription purchase and validation) runs the purchase for us: it tells the app which plans to offer, carries out the purchase through Apple, and tells the app whether Premium is active. Adapty also records app opens and which set of plans was requested, helps us diagnose purchase problems, and measures attribution for our own Apple Ads. The Adapty code inside the app starts when you open the app, not when you first see the Premium screen. It sends Adapty:

  • identifiers for this installation: an identifier the Adapty code creates for itself, Adapty's own profile identifier, and Apple's identifier for vendor (IDFV) – an identifier that is the same for all apps from one developer on your phone;
  • the identifier Mixpanel uses for this installation (see “Usage analytics”), so that Adapty's records and Mixpanel's can be joined;
  • your purchases: Apple's confirmation of each purchase and restore, and the state of Premium;
  • which set of plans the app asked for, and that the app was opened;
  • technical details of the phone and the installation: device model, iOS version, screen size, language and region settings, time zone, the country of the App Store you use, app version and build, the Adapty code's version, when the app was installed, the time of each request, the tracking-permission status reported by iOS (the app never asks for it), and a standard web browser identification string;
  • technical logs of the Adapty code, such as request identifiers, outcomes, errors and timings;
  • Apple's Search Ads attribution token – see “Advertising and tracking” below.

Your IP address and your country. Adapty's servers see your IP address with every request, as any server does. We have switched off the Adapty code's own collection of your IP address: the app does not look it up or write it into your Adapty profile. Adapty can still work out an approximate country from the address of the request and keep it in your profile. That is not your location from GPS or Wi-Fi – the app never asks for location – but it is a rough guess of where you are, and we list it here for that reason.

Usage analytics

We use Mixpanel (usage analytics, EU servers) to see how the app is used, so that we can tell where it breaks and whether people find it worth paying for. The app sends events to Mixpanel's European endpoint (api-eu.mixpanel.com), for a Mixpanel project that stores its data in the European Union.

What is counted. One event each time something of these kinds happens, with a few plain details attached:

  • opening the app for the first time, opening it again, coming back to it, and finishing the introduction;
  • picking a file: whether it came from Photos or Files, and its size and length as a rough range (for example “10–50 MB”, “2–10 minutes”), never the exact figure;
  • an extraction: that it started, which format and quality you chose, whether you trimmed it, whether it was a ringtone, and when it finished, the length of the result and how long the work took, again as rough ranges;
  • opening the Share menu, or saving a file to Files;
  • the Premium screen: being shown it and where from, which plan you tapped, whether a purchase or a restore went through, was cancelled, is waiting for approval or failed, and closing the screen; using up the free file;
  • the app asking iOS to show Apple's “rate this app” window;
  • a failure to link this installation's Mixpanel identifier to Adapty.

Event values come only from fixed lists, numbers and error codes – a file name, a path or anything you typed is never put into an event. When something fails, the event carries a short technical code for the failure, never the error message itself. A failure because the phone ran out of storage is not sent at all.

Attached to events: the app version and build, iOS version, the language and region set on your phone, whether the copy of the app is a test build, and what Mixpanel's code adds by itself – device model, operating system, screen size, whether the phone is on Wi-Fi, the type of mobile network and, where iOS still provides it, the mobile carrier's name. Most of these, without the network details, are also kept as a small profile for this installation.

The identifier. Mixpanel knows this installation by a random identifier created on your phone the first time the app runs. It is not your IDFV and not the advertising identifier. The app gives this identifier to Adapty (see above), which is what lets the two services' records be joined.

Location. For events sent directly by the app, Mixpanel's IP-based geolocation is disabled. The app sends the region selected on your phone. Purchase events sent separately by Adapty (below) may include the App Store country and a country inferred by Adapty from the connection IP.

Purchase events from Adapty. Adapty sends our Mixpanel project its own records of purchase events – for example, a subscription starting, renewing or ending – under the same Mixpanel identifier. These come from Adapty's side, not from the app, so they can arrive even when the app is not open, and they carry what Adapty holds about the purchase and your profile, such as the product, the price and our proceeds, and the store transaction identifiers.

Advertising and tracking

  • There are no ads in the app, and no advertising SDK.
  • The app never asks for permission to track you, and does not read the advertising identifier (IDFA). We have switched IDFA collection off in the Adapty code as well.
  • We use Apple Ads attribution to measure our own campaigns. If available, the Adapty code sends an Apple Ads attribution token when synchronizing a profile. Apple designed this token so that Apple can attribute an install to a view or tap on an ad for the app on the App Store; it works without the IDFA and without the tracking permission.
  • We do not use your app activity to target ads across other companies' apps or websites, or sell it to data brokers.

Why we use this data

  • Providing Premium – selling it, checking whether it is active, restoring it, and the free first file: this is necessary to perform our contract with you.
  • Usage analytics, diagnostics and Apple Ads attribution: our legitimate interest in keeping the app working, understanding which features are used and whether our own advertising pays for itself. We limit this to the data described above and never include your files.
  • Answering your messages: our legitimate interest in helping you.

Your Library is eligible for device backup, on purpose. It can be restored if the backup you restore contains AudioConv's data. The cost is that deleting a file in the app does not reach into backups already made: those copies remain until the backup itself is replaced or deleted, which is between you and Apple. The keychain mark for the free file contains nothing about you – only the fact that the free file is used. It is not synced through iCloud Keychain. It normally survives reinstallation and may migrate through an encrypted backup. Reinstalling AudioConv is not a reliable way to reset the free file.

Deleting a file in the Library (swipe left, then Delete) removes it from the phone; copies you saved to Files stay. Deleting the app removes its local files and preferences, including Adapty's and Mixpanel's identifiers on the phone. It does not remove the keychain mark, anything already in a backup, copies you saved or sent elsewhere, or anything already sent to Adapty or Mixpanel. Apple controls the identifier for vendor, which may remain available to other apps from the same developer.

How long data is kept

  • On your phone: until you delete it, as described above.
  • Mixpanel: Mixpanel's standard retention for events is two years from the event date. That is the service's standard, not a period we have set or can guarantee. Installation profiles are kept under Mixpanel's rules for our plan unless deleted earlier.
  • Adapty: purchase records are kept while needed to verify or restore Premium, and for any retention period the law requires.
  • Your e-mails to us: kept while we need them to answer you and follow up, then deleted.
  • Apple keeps its own record of what you bought from it, under Apple's privacy policy.

Your choices and your rights

Your files

Delete any file in the Library, or delete the app to remove them all. Copies in your backups, in Files or wherever you shared them are yours to manage.

Adapty and Mixpanel

There is no setting in the app that stops Adapty or Mixpanel. You can object to this processing, or ask us to delete what was collected, by writing to us (below). If you keep using the app, collection continues: the app sends events while it runs, and Adapty sends purchase events on its own when a subscription renews or ends.

Your subscription

Cancel it in Apple's subscription settings, and ask Apple for refunds, as described under “Premium and purchases”. Asking us to delete data does not cancel a subscription.

Your rights

If the GDPR applies to you, you may request access to your data, its correction or erasure, restriction of its processing or, where applicable, a portable copy of it. You may object to processing based on our legitimate interests. You may also complain to a data protection supervisory authority, including in the country where you live or work.

Making a privacy request

E-mail quicklysearch.bot@gmail.com with “privacy request” in the subject. We respond without undue delay and within one month. If the request is complex or we receive several requests, we may need up to two additional months; if so, we will tell you why within the first month.

Here is the difficulty, stated in advance. There is no account, so your e-mail address matches nothing at Adapty or Mixpanel, and the app does not show you the identifiers their records are keyed to. If we need more information to locate your records or verify your request, we will explain what is needed. An approximate installation date or plan name alone is not enough for us to disclose or erase a record – it could belong to somebody else. If we cannot identify your records, we will explain why. Deleting the app does not cancel a subscription or erase records already held by our providers. We cannot reach your iPhone backups, files you shared, or Apple's record of your purchases.
